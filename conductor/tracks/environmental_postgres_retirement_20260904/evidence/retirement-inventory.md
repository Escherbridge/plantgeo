# Retirement inventory — every environmental PostgreSQL relation, its filler, its readers

Worker A3, wave A, read-only. Built by reading the declarative tree
(`services/agri-data-service/db/agri/**`), every `drizzle/*.sql` migration, the 59-lane
`LANE_SPECS` table in `execution/job_executor_service.py`, the 32-slug Parquet
`LANE_REGISTRATIONS` in `pipeline/parquet/lane_registry.py`, and a repository-wide grep for
readers across `src/` (Next.js), `services/agri-data-service/src/agri_data_service/sql/agent/**`
+ `agent/tools.py`, `infra/martin/martin.yaml` + the tile function bodies, the CLI, and tests.
Every claim below carries a `file:line`. Where I could not find one, the row says so instead of
guessing — see "What I could not determine".

**Scope note — `public.priority_zones`, `public.strategy_requests`, `public.request_votes`, and
`geo.poi` are community/basemap features (DBSCAN-clustered strategy requests, POI directory), not
environmental source data, and are out of this inventory's scope the same way the feed/social
Drizzle tables are. `geo.osm_*` (buildings/roads/waterways/landuse/pois) is OSM basemap data, also
out of scope.**

**Structural finding that changes how "drop after Parquet proof" reads below:** `geo.features` is
one polymorphic table holding **seven of the eight D4 layers** — weather-observations,
fire-perimeters, vegetation NDVI, sensors, evacuation-zones, watersheds, and burn-severity — keyed
by `layer_id` (`db/agri` has no equivalent; the split lives entirely in `drizzle/`). Only drought
has its own dedicated table (`geo.drought_areas`). This means `geo.features` (and the geometry
dimension underneath it, `geo.geometry`) cannot be dropped layer-by-layer at the relation level —
it is gated on the *last* of its seven layers to clear parity + reader-move, even though each
layer's *rows* in it can be proven independently. I have written one row for `geo.features`
rather than seven duplicate rows, and noted per-layer evidence in its Notes column.
